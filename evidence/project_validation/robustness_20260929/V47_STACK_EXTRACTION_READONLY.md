## What the recorded lift does

All 19 target trays are initially above another tray. The lower tray starts with four table contacts and the pair with four tray-to-tray contacts. At the last saved frame, **19/19 lower trays have zero table contacts** and have risen **15.1–90.7 mm (median 29.3 mm)** relative to their first saved frame. The target and lower tray move nearly together: terminal target-minus-lower vertical displacement is **−1.44 to +0.83 mm, median +0.75 mm**. At the saved 20 Hz frames, the lower tray's two `gripper_contact_force` values are both zero. This favors tray-to-tray drag/entrainment over a persistent direct gripper grasp of the lower tray, but 20 Hz samples cannot rule out 2 ms transient robot contact.

For example, scene `airport_0001_20270917`, top `tray_2` on `tray_1`, starts lift at frame 414. By frame 430, top and lower are about 51.2 and 51.0 mm above their first-frame heights, respectively, and the lower has no table contact. Scene `airport_0000_20270916` has a previous ordinary-tray operation; its stacked top lift starts at frame 1238 and ends with the lower 29.3 mm above the first-frame height. The 19 terminal scenes come from the five complex blocks; these observations are not an independent success-rate sample.

## Geometric vertical counterfactual

In a separate `MjData` at the saved start-of-stacked-lift `qpos/qvel`, `mj_forward` recomputed contacts after translating **only the top tray** vertically. At 0 mm there are four tray-pair contacts per scene, with max penetration about 0.05–0.06 mm. At **+1 mm, zero pair contacts in all 19**. The additional static positions +2, +5, +10, +20, +30, +50, +70, +100, and +150 mm also have zero pair contacts. The initial contact normals' absolute vertical component is about 0.178; horizontal component about 0.984; MuJoCo contact friction is 0.8. Thus a collision-free vertical separation exists *geometrically*, while the real dual-grip lift remains in the contact-coupled branch. The static translation does not predict the forces or controller motion needed to leave that branch. `mj_forward` here performs no `mj_step`, so this audit cannot measure dynamic normal/friction impulses or certify an executable robot trajectory.

This evidence does **not** support merely weakening `multiple_trays_lifted`, lowering tray friction, moving qpos directly, or replaying the same two-hand lift more slowly as a claimed fix. The existing saved lift already goes upward yet entrains the lower tray; static clearance alone is insufficient. The previous horizontal static sweep showed 2 mm lateral motion deepened side contact, and 5 mm breached the 3 mm penetration limit in the two representative scenes.

## Private short dynamics control comparison

I also restored scene 1's saved lift-start `qpos/qvel/qacc_warmstart` into an isolated `MjData` and replayed recorded actuator `ctrl` for only the lift segment at 2 ms simulation steps. The unchanged-control replay ends at top/lower rises of **50.1/50.6 mm**, close to the stored **50.5/51.0 mm**. Its lower table contact disappears at 0.21 s and the top/lower pair never loses its four contacts. This near reproduction makes a bounded *diagnostic* control comparison useful; it is still not a normal FSM episode, and feedback, camera, velocity, and force guards are not reconstructed.

Two private variations kept the same collision bodies, friction, and guards' numerical thresholds. Repeating each saved control target for twice as long (half-rate target progression) delays lower table-contact loss to 0.41 s but still moves top/lower together: at the first sampled top rise ≥3 mm, lower rise is 3.48 mm versus top 3.53 mm. Holding an early pre-lift control target for 0.8 s delays lower-contact loss to 1.01 s; after continuation, at first top rise ≥3 mm, lower rise is 3.02 mm versus top 3.07 mm. Pair contacts never break in either variation. Peak tray-pair normal force is about **20.6–21.2 N** across the three variants; accumulated normal impulse is **46.7 N·s** original, **83.5 N·s** half-rate, **96.3 N·s** pause, with different durations. These are simulated contact forces in the private replay, not forces measured in the saved trace. Numeric per-step evidence: `runs/failure_videos/stack_short_profiles_snapshot.json`; script: `runs/failure_videos/audit_stack_short_profiles.py`.

Thus slowing the original two-hand command or pausing before lift did not produce the 1 mm relative separation in this representative state. It only postponed the same table-contact loss. This one-scene diagnostic does not prove that every conceivable velocity or acceleration profile fails, but it gives no basis to promote such a parameter-only change.

## Specific next experiment

The one physically credible route worth screening is **asymmetric, supported extraction**: hold the top tray with one hand while the other hand supplies a measured downward/holding contact to the lower tray against the table; then lift the top only 1–3 mm to break the four pair contacts, verify separation and lower support, release the lower hand, and transition to the existing two-hand grasp/carry. This is a hypothesis, not an established available primitive. It should start with a bounded checkpoint experiment on scene 1 (then scene 0), **not** a 400-scene run. Before allowing even the 3 mm move, a private plan must show a stable one-hand top grip, a reachable lower-tray contact that does not collide with the top or camera, and full swept robot/tray collision checks. If those cannot be shown, reject the candidate without physical execution.

During a real 2 ms-step micro-lift, record top/lower Z, lower table contacts and normal load, all tray-pair normal/tangential impulses, both hands' tray contact forces, wrist-camera/collision metrics, and penetration. The diagnostic passes only if top-minus-lower rise reaches at least 1 mm while lower-table contacts stay present, lower-tray absolute rise stays within 2 mm, global penetration stays ≤3 mm, and existing arm force, speed, camera, and collision guards hold. After the lower hand releases, the top must remain supported by a valid grasp; subsequent normal lift and complete task validation remain mandatory. In particular, the current multi-tray guard checks while `holding` is set; any pre-holding one-hand maneuver needs an explicit lower-tray support/height guard rather than relying on the existing check. Do not reduce friction, disable collision, or increase the multi-tray tolerance for this experiment.

If single-hand retention and lower hold-down cannot both be planned under those rules, stop this branch. No current data justify a production change or any success-rate increase.
